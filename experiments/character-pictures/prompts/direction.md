How to draw it:
- Every part named above lies exactly where its stroke lies, with the stroke's shape, length and angle. Draw those parts as bold, solid black brush strokes of regular script, about as wide as a written stroke, so that the black strokes alone read clearly as the character $character. Someone who knows $character must recognise it at a glance; someone who does not must see the object.
- Keep every stroke of $character exactly as it is written. Do not add, drop, merge or split a stroke; do not bend, curl or add a hook to one; do not turn a stroke into a flame, a drop, a dot, a branch or anything else. Nothing grows out of a stroke. Where two strokes touch or cross in $character, they touch or cross in the picture; where they stand apart, they stay apart.
- Draw the object over and around the strokes, so that the strokes are its parts. Do not draw the object beside the character.
- Bold solid black belongs to the strokes alone: they are the heaviest, darkest marks in the picture. The rest of the picture is lighter but clearly visible, in soft colours, never so faint that the object disappears. Thin outlines, fine lines and greys are welcome, but nothing else may be a thick black mark that could be taken for a stroke, above all where it touches a stroke or lies inside the character. If the strokes were hidden, nothing left should look like part of $character.
- No text of any kind: do not write, print, paint or carve $character, or any other character, letter or number, anywhere in the picture. No stroke-order numbers, arrows or labels.
- The object fills the square the way the character fills its box. No frame or circle around the character, and no backdrop such as a page, a sign or a wall. The background is white or a very pale wash, with nothing in it that looks like a stroke.
- A clear, simple, friendly illustration, like a page of a picture book that teaches Chinese characters: not elaborate.
