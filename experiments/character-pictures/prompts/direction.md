How to draw it:
- Every part named above lies exactly where its stroke lies, with the stroke's shape, length and angle. Draw those parts as bold, solid black brush strokes of regular script, about as wide as a written stroke, so that the black strokes alone read clearly as the character $character. Someone who knows $character must recognise it at a glance; someone who does not must see the object.
- Keep every stroke of $character exactly as it is written. Do not add, drop, merge or split a stroke; do not bend, curl or add a hook to one; do not turn a stroke into a flame, a drop, a dot, a branch or anything else. Draw every stroke whole, to its full length. Where two strokes touch or cross in $character, they touch or cross in the picture; where they stand apart, they stay apart.
- Keep each stroke's direction: a slanting stroke stays slanting at its angle, a straight one stays straight, a dot keeps its tilt. Keep the character's proportions: each part of $character keeps its own size and its place in the box, so a part that is narrower, shorter or set higher than another stays so. Do not make the character wider, narrower, taller or shorter.
- The strokes stay plain written strokes. Do not work them into the drawing: nothing grows out of a stroke, so no branch, root, leaf, flame, finger or line extends from a stroke's end or side, and no texture, bark, grain or shading is painted onto a stroke to make it look like part of the object. The object is drawn behind and around the strokes, and each of its parts sits on its own stroke, lined up with it, so that the stroke is visibly that part. Do not draw the object beside the character.
- The strokes are on top of everything. Nothing in the picture covers a stroke; at most it may touch a stroke's edge. Ground, water or grass stays below the character's lowest stroke unless a stroke is named as that ground.
- Bold solid black belongs to the strokes alone: they are the heaviest, darkest marks in the picture. The rest of the picture is lighter but clearly visible, in soft colours, never so faint that the object disappears. Thin outlines, fine lines and greys are welcome, but nothing else may be a thick black mark or a dark shape that could be taken for a stroke, above all where it touches a stroke or lies inside the character. No stray dot, spot or speck near the strokes. If the strokes were hidden, nothing left should look like part of $character.
- $character appears once. Do not repeat it, or any part of it, elsewhere in the picture. No text of any kind: do not write, print, paint or carve $character, or any other character, letter or number, anywhere in the picture. No stroke-order numbers, arrows or labels.
- Where the picture shows a person or an animal, it is drawn whole and friendly: a person always has a face with eyes, a nose and a mouth, and is dressed in simple clothes. Add no person or animal that the description does not name.
- The object fills the square the way the character fills its box. No frame or circle around the character, and no backdrop such as a page, a sign or a wall. The background is white or a very pale wash, with nothing in it that looks like a stroke.
- A clear, simple, friendly illustration, like a page of a picture book that teaches Chinese characters: not elaborate.
