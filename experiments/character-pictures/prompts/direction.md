How to draw it:
- Every part named above lies exactly where its stroke lies, with the stroke's shape, length and angle. Draw those parts in bold black brush ink, about as wide as a written stroke, so that the black parts alone still read clearly as the character $character. Someone who knows $character must recognise it at a glance; someone who does not must see the object.
- Everything else the object needs — a face, hands, leaves, sky, water — is pale watercolour in soft colours, much lighter and thinner than the ink, so that it never competes with the ink or looks like an extra stroke.
- The character must come only from the object's own parts. Do not write, print, paint or carve the character $character, or any other character, letter or number, anywhere in the picture, and do not draw a scene with the character laid over it.
- The object fills the square the way the character fills its box. The background is white or a very pale wash, with nothing in it that looks like a stroke.
- A clear, simple, friendly illustration, like a page of a picture book that teaches Chinese characters.
