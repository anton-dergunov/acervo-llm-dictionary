# Round 1: the owner's notes

These are the owner's spoken notes from judging round 1 on the tablet, edited for reading. The opinions
are the owner's. Each blind letter is followed by the picture it was, from [`key.json`](key.json): model
(NB2 = Nano Banana 2, Pro = Nano Banana Pro, klein = FLUX.2 klein), way and take. Corrections added
afterwards, after checking a picture against the pinned strokes, are marked *Checked:*.

## Overall

The task is worth continuing. Every character had at least one picture the owner liked, and often
several. Several times a picture would have been the favourite but for one defect, and most of those
defects look as if they come from the prompt, so the next step is to tighten the prompt rather than to
stop. Imperfect pictures are expected: the model is asked several times, and the best one is chosen.

## What went wrong, and what it asks of the prompt

- **The strokes must be the black ink, and nothing else may look like a stroke.**
  - Black shoes at the ends of 人's and 从's legs blurred where the strokes end.
  - So did a solid black tree outline, its roots, and strokes added at its base (休), a black mouth drawn inside 口, and a dark cloud above 雨.
  - Dark outlines in themselves are fine. The trouble is a thick black mark that could be read as part of the character.
- **A coloured reference came back as coloured ink.** The edit way's reference picture had each stroke in its own colour. Several edit-way pictures drew the strokes in those colours, and then it is hard to tell stroke from background. The reference should be black.
- **Stroke-order numbers.** Some pictures numbered the strokes (从, 休, 月, 雨, 竹, 羊), and all of them were text-way pictures, whose prompt lists the strokes as "Stroke 1, …". Stroke order has its own feature (Write); the picture should not show it.
- **The character changed.**
  - A stroke was replaced by a flame (火), a raindrop or a dot (雨), or a branch (竹, 木).
  - Strokes were added (休, 册, 羊) or dropped (日, 口).
  - A curly re-styling changed the shape (水).
  - The picture must keep the character exactly. An idea can be lovely and still fail on this.
- **Hooks.** 木, 林 and 森 often got a hook at the foot of the trunk, which the character does not have.
- **Strokes that should join, floating free.** The inner lines of 月 and 明 and the joins in 竹 and 凸 were sometimes detached. A detached stroke may make a different character.
- **The object beside the character, not on it.** The owner prefers a picture where the strokes are the object's parts: legs that are 人's strokes, a person leaning into 休's tree, sun and moon inside 明's two halves. A figure standing next to the character, or not aligned with it, is weaker.
- **Too pale and too elaborate are both failures.** A picture so pale that only the character is left adds nothing. One so busy that it competes with the strokes hides the character.
- **Frames and backdrops.** A circle around the character (日) or a book page behind it (口) adds outlines that compete with the strokes.
- **Style.** Brush-written, hand-drawn strokes were usually preferred, and the Chinese-style colours were liked. The owner was unsure whether the squarer, printed form (as the web UI shows it) is equally right; see the README's answer.
- **The "remember" question** is too subjective to label apart from the 1–5 score, which already captures it. It is dropped from round 2. "Strokes" and "depicts" are kept, though they too are hard to judge.

## For the full run (later)

At around 3,300 characters, judging should be quicker:
- One character at a time, several pictures at once.
- Each picture marked **good**, **usable but not perfect**, or **not usable**.
- A regeneration step that first redraws the characters with nothing usable, then those with only usable pictures.

Round 2 keeps round 1's page, minus the "remember" question.
