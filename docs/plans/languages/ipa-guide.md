# IPA guide · what each symbol in a transcription means

**Status:** built (`web/src/ipa/`), with two layout choices still being tried by hand (below). The
table and the recordings were prepared in
[`experiments/ipa-sounds/`](../../../experiments/ipa-sounds/README.md). Left: the owner picks a
phone layout and a marks layout; then the losers and the trial switch go, the winner goes into the
prototype (`acervo.css` with `styles.css`), and this plan becomes `docs/features/ipa-guide.md`.

A learner who has never read IPA sees `/tʰu˧˥ ʂu˥ ku̯an˨˩˦/` and learns nothing from it. The guide
explains a transcription where it is shown: tap it, and a panel lists each sound in it with a plain
description, the nearest English sound, and a recording.

## Interaction

- **A tap or click on the transcription opens a panel**, the same on every device. There is no hover
  tooltip: the tablet comes first, and a tooltip cannot hold a play button.
- **One row per sound**, in the order the transcription has them: the symbol large, its plain
  description (`how`), the English "like …" or, when English has no such sound, a word in a language
  that has it (`heard`), and ▶ for the chosen recording.
- **Tones** are drawn as a pitch line from their tone letters and named: `˥˩` is "falling", and for a
  Mandarin word also "4th tone". A run of tone letters the table does not name is described from its
  levels ("from mid to high").
- **Explanations are in English only.** No translation is planned.
- **Where it opens:** everywhere a transcription is shown, which is two places: your word's article
  and a dictionary entry for a word you do not have. The cards view shows no transcription on
  purpose, and lists, review, stories and the map show none.
- **Placement:** on a tablet or desktop, a popover under the transcription. A sound shown twice in a
  transcription is one row.

## Being tried

The owner chose to decide two things by trying them on the devices, so both ship behind a
device-local switch in Settings ▸ Reading ("IPA guide (trial)", `ipa/trial.ts`):

- **On a phone:** a bottom sheet over the lower two thirds (the default), the full screen, or inline
  in the article as the transcription row's last line.
- **Marks:** inside the letter's row (the default: `u̯` is one row with a line for ̯; stress marks are
  lit in the panel's copy of the transcription, with one line each saying what they mean), or a row
  of their own after the letter, stress too.

## Splitting a transcription into sounds

IPA is one alphabet, so the split does not depend on the word's language. The splitter is pure
TypeScript and is told no language:

- a base letter keeps the combining marks and modifier letters after it (`ʰ ʲ ʷ ˞ ː`, `̃`, `̯` …):
  `tʰ` and `u̯` are one unit each; longest match first, so a letter with a recorded mark (`tʰ`, `ɑ̃`)
  is matched whole;
- a precomposed letter the table does not hold (`é`, `ã`) is read as its letter and its mark;
- two letters joined by a tie bar that are not a listed affricate (`k͡p`) are one sound of two parts;
- a character the table does not know is a row that says so, never dropped;
- a run of tone letters is one unit;
- an affricate is one unit, with or without a tie bar: `ts`, `tɕ`, `ʈʂ`, `t͡s`. Always grouping them is
  harmless; English "cats" showing `ts` as one sound is still right;
- aliases are mapped before looking up: `g` → `ɡ`, `:` → `ː`, `ʦ` → `ts`;
- the slashes or brackets around a transcription, spaces and `.` are not rows.

## Pinyin

For a Mandarin word the panel also lists, per syllable, its Pinyin initial and final with the IPA
each stands for and an English hint. That answers the question a learner of Mandarin actually has
("why is *zh* written ʈʂ?") and keeps Pinyin's surprises (*q* is never "kw", *-i* after *sh* is not
"ee") next to the transcription.

- `ipa/pinyinParts.ts` is a TypeScript twin of `mandarin_ipa._initial_and_final`, pinned by shared
  vectors (`web/src/testFixtures/pinyinPartsVectors.json`, all 411 syllables, read by both test
  suites), so the panel and the stored transcription never disagree. The server spells finals out
  (`iou`, `uei`, `uen`); the panel shows the rows as Pinyin writes them (`iu`, `ui`, `un`).
- Every final the server can reach has a row; `io` (哟 yō) and `ueng` (嗡 wēng) were added for that.
- The Pinyin examples are real syllables, so they could be voiced by the app's own speech instead of
  Commons recordings; the chart sounds cannot be, since no voice says a sound on its own.

## Data

- **The table is written once, by hand, and committed**: `web/src/ipa/symbols.json` (chart
  consonants, affricates written without a tie bar, vowels with `ɚ ɝ ɫ`, diacritics, stress and
  length, tone letters and marks, the Pinyin initials, finals and tones, and the recordings' credits).
  JSON because `yaml.ts` is the only place the app reads YAML. It is never generated by a model per
  word. It loads with the panel, on the first tap, so it is not in the bundle every page loads.
- **It is tested** (`tests/unit/test_ipa_table.py`): each Pinyin row states the IPA `mandarin_ipa.py`
  writes, every final the server can cut a syllable into has a row, every character Mandarin IPA can
  produce is in the table, every `sound` is a file and every file is played, and every file is
  credited.
- **A marked letter plays its own recording when there is one** (aspirated `tʰ`, French nasal
  `ɑ̃` …, the table's `combinations`), and otherwise its bare letter's, with the mark's text saying
  what differs. Marks with no sound of their own (stress, length, tone marks, a diacritic alone)
  have text and no recording.
- **Recordings come from openly licensed sources** (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA), cleaned and
  levelled (the experiment's "Third round"): 165 files for 184 cards, **1.72 MB** of Ogg Opus, the
  codec and setting stored pronunciations use, each named by a digest of its bytes.
- **They ship with the app, not with the vocabulary.** The same for every account and never edited,
  so they are not records: no replica, no server route, no sync. They are static files in the build,
  precached by the service worker with the rest of the app (`ogg` added to the precache patterns),
  so every device has all of them after one install, offline from the first tap, at 1.7 MB once.
  They live in `web/src/ipa/sounds/`, committed, so any checkout builds a release; `npm run test:pwa`
  fails if one is missing from the precache list. The make dialogs' pictures (`webp`) were added to
  the same patterns: they were not precached, so they were missing offline.
  Fetching each on first play instead would leave a sound unheard offline until it had been played
  online, the one thing an offline-first read must not do. A digest name means a new build re-downloads
  only a file that changed.
- **Played as a blob, like every other recording in the app.** Safari asks for audio in byte ranges,
  which a precached response does not answer; the panel fetches the file (from the precache) and
  plays an object URL, as `mediaStore.ts`' clips are played, and stops other players through
  `pronunciation.registerPlayer`.
- **Credits:** CC BY and CC BY-SA require attribution, so Settings ▸ About ▸ Sounds in the IPA guide
  lists, per file, what it plays for, the author, the licence with its link, the Commons page and how
  the file was changed. BY-SA also keeps a changed file under its licence, which the list says.
